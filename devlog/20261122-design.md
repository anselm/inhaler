# The design, and what was taken from four earlier aggregators

22 November 2026. Claude (Fable 5.2). See `20261122-init.md` for the ask.

## What the earlier projects had worked out

Two agents read the four projects and reported with file and line. In short:

**Cortes and Salt Spring** are one small codebase (Salt Spring is Cortes with everything
about the place moved into a `places/<id>.json`). They have the widest set of sources:
Google News search, local papers' feeds, iNaturalist, Wikipedia, Flickr, Mastodon,
OpenStreetMap. Their fetching is a timer and a loop: no backoff, no conditional requests,
no hashing of content, nothing for items that disappear. Their notes say what was walled
off: Facebook groups ("where island chatter actually lives and are unscrapeable"), X
(a reader that waits for a token nobody supplied), Reddit from a data centre, and no
calendar or council agendas at all. Their placing of items taught three things: a
hand-made gazetteer matched by longest name works; scattering unplaced items around a
centre "put them all in Hague Lake"; a bare search word pulls in the wrong Cortés.

**Matchmaker** has the best fetching: a source is `{fetch, normalize}` in one file per
kind, defined in a config file and overridden in the database, with its health beside it;
failure backs off as the interval doubled per failure, capped at 48 hours, with jitter; an
item that disappears is marked gone, never after a failed or empty fetch; a probe turns a
name into a source definition only after something answers.

**The ontology scraper** has the best pipeline: each document carries a state per phase,
and a phase claims documents by that state with a lease. Its devlogs carry the costly
lessons. A billing error was counted as the document's failure and 1,474 documents were
lost for good. Claiming oldest first starved the present. Spending on one phase went
unmeasured at about ten dollars a day. A shared key hid which project was spending. It
measured models: a small hosted model at a fifth of a cent a document kept nine tenths of
precision; in matchmaker's tests the local `gpt-oss:20b` gave valid JSON every time.

Nobody had built: per-host spacing together with conditional requests and gone-marking in
one place; social sources that work; or a fact as the unit kept.

## What this takes and what it does differently

Taken: the place as a config file (Salt Spring); one small file per source kind with one
shape (matchmaker); backoff doubled per failure with a cap and jitter, and gone-marking
only after a full successful listing (matchmaker); conditional requests (ontology); a
state per pass on each row, newest claimed first, and "no model available" leaving work
untouched (ontology, the fix for its two worst days); the gazetteer matched by longest
name and no scattering (the islands); the tier that read an item recorded on it
(matchmaker); a local model first.

Different:

- **The unit is a fact, not a card and not an entity.** The islands kept items to show;
  the ontology kept entities and relations. A simulation wants neither. It wants "the
  council voted on X on this date at this place, according to this source": something
  that can sit on a timeline and a map and be counted. An item is kept too, as where the
  fact came from.
- **One store file, no server.** SQLite through Node's own module. The others need Mongo.
  A beat can be inhaled on a laptop.
- **X through FixTweet.** It gives an account's posts without a key. It has no search, so
  a place is followed by the accounts that speak about it and the digest decides which
  posts concern it.
- **Checks that are not the model's word.** The ontology notes say "confidence is
  decorative". Here the quoted words must be found in the source text, a date must parse,
  an issue's fact ids must exist, and how a fact was placed is recorded.
- **A gazetteer drawn from OpenStreetMap for the beat's box**, in place of a hand-made
  list, so a new place needs no list.

## One inhaler for all of them?

Anselm's question. My answer is yes in direction, and not by moving them now.

What all five projects do alike is the dull half: knowing sources and their health,
fetching politely, keeping what was fetched with its hash and its going, running passes
that claim work, calling models in tiers with a ledger, geocoding with a cache. That half
is here, and written with no place or subject in it. What differs is the lens put on an
item: the islands want a card, matchmaker wants a job posting scored against asks, the
ontology wants entities and relations, a simulation wants facts. A lens is a digesting
pass. So the shape that would serve all of them is this program with its digest pass made
pluggable: the fetch side shared, one lens per client, each lens's results in its own
table.

What argues for waiting: this is one day old and has read one town. Matchmaker's 161
sources and twelve kinds are worth more than this code; moving them is its room's work
and wants this to have run for some weeks first. The step I would take next is small and
tests the idea: give matchmaker's `rss` and this `feed` one shared shape and see whether
a job feed and a news feed really are the same thing to the fetch side.

## What the first run showed

One beat, Sausalito; eight sources (two papers' feeds for the town, Google News search,
Weather Service alerts, four X accounts); the local model `gpt-oss:20b` for every pass.

| | |
|---|---|
| items fetched | 161, in about four seconds |
| items the digest judged to concern Sausalito | 84 |
| facts drawn | 128 (64 events, 18 decisions, 12 incidents, 11 measurements, 8 plans, 7 opinions, 6 proposals, 2 other) |
| facts with a date | 106 |
| facts whose quoted words were found in the source | 76 |
| facts put at a point | 27 (6 by the whole name, 21 by a name inside the phrase) |
| facts about the town as a whole | 81 |
| facts naming a place the gazetteer did not know | 20 |
| time to digest | 13 minutes, about 5 seconds an item; no failures |
| issues read from 73 recent facts | 7, in 19 seconds |

The seven issues, as the model titled them: flooding threats and defensive upgrades;
the Varda Landing dike and private flood-control projects; waterfront zoning and
Measure X; housing development at Phillips Field; ferry service disruptions and weather;
real-time tidal monitoring and a public dashboard; an AI tool for storm preparedness.
Set beside the research done by hand that morning, it found the flooding at Gate 5,
Measure X and the ferry, and it found two things the hand pass did not have: the county's
tidal sensor at Waldo Point Harbor (installed 9 November 2026, by the fact's own date) and
a news story about an AI tool for storm preparedness in Sausalito.

What is wrong with it, seen in the output:

- **It has read nothing of the city's own pages.** sausalito.gov has no feed, so the
  council's agendas and staff reports, which the hand pass leaned on, are not here. The
  budget, the housing numbers and the shoreline plan's figures are absent for that reason.
- **Only 27 of 128 facts have a point.** Most news says "Sausalito" and no more. Twenty
  named a place the gazetteer lacks; I have not looked at which.
- **Events of no civic weight are facts too**: an art show's opening hours are four facts.
  Nothing ranks a fact yet.
- **A private person was named** (an artist, in a studio listing) although the digest is
  told not to. The instruction is not enough; it needs a check.
- **A third of the facts have no quote that could be found in the text.** Many are from
  Google News items, which are only a headline.
- **Two facts were placed at "Marin City" for a school district's name**, which is a
  wrong reading of an organisation as a place.
- **Nothing was measured.** No one has read a sample of items by hand to say what the
  model missed or got wrong. The counts above are what it produced, not how good it is.
- The same story from several outlets was digested once per outlet.
